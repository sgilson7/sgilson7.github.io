Course materials

Everything I taught from and everything students worked on, as the files they were given. The CSC 226 set is one study: two assignments built to defeat a chatbot, with a session between them on how to catch one being wrong.

I was the instructor of record, and the university has given IRB approval to publish these materials for research. Every file is served from this site.

CSC 226 Discrete Mathematics

  • AI Literacy for Mathematical Reasoning Lecture · PDF, 18 pages · Summer 2026

    The reading that sets up the class session. It works through how a chatbot produces mathematical text one token at a time, the three training stages and what none of them stores, and then the three ways a chatbot's proof goes wrong, each with the check that catches it.

    Learning objectives
    • Describe how large language models generate mathematical text.
    • Detect hallucinations in chatbot proofs using the autopsy procedure (labels 0-6).
    • Improve weak prompts using the four parts of a strong prompt.
    • Collaborate with a chatbot using the collaboration procedure (labels 1-6), and evaluate the system behind it.

    Teaches: Hallucination Autopsy Procedure, Strong Prompt Structure, Collaborative Prompting, Axiom Reversion, Invalid Syntactic Shortcut, Spatial or Counting Miscount

    From Perturbed Assignments and Subgoal-Labeled AI Literacy Instruction in Undergraduate Discrete Mathematics

  • Working with AI on Proofs Exercises · PDF, 16 pages · Summer 2026

    The packet students worked during the session, Exercises A to C, one per procedure. Each label appears three times: in a reference box, in a worked example, and in the task the student does, which is what makes them subgoal labels rather than a checklist.

    Learning objectives
    • Detect and diagnose hallucinations in chatbot proofs using the autopsy procedure (labels 0-6).
    • Improve weak prompts using the four parts of a strong prompt.
    • Collaborate with a chatbot on an unfamiliar counting problem using the collaboration procedure (labels 1-6).

    Teaches: Hallucination Autopsy Procedure, Strong Prompt Structure, Collaborative Prompting, Subgoal Label

    From Perturbed Assignments and Subgoal-Labeled AI Literacy Instruction in Undergraduate Discrete Mathematics

  • Lab 3, Part 1 Assignment · PDF, 12 pages · Summer 2026

    The first observation. Questions 1 to 3 are the perturbed counting, induction and predicate logic questions, validated against Gemini 3.5 Flash, which students could reach under a university licence. A chatbot was permitted on the condition that the chat log came in with the work.

    Teaches: One-Shot GenAI Resistance, iterative perturbation process, Spatial Injection, Axiomatic Replacement, Contextual Embedding

    From Perturbed Assignments and Subgoal-Labeled AI Literacy Instruction in Undergraduate Discrete Mathematics

  • Final Exam Review Lab, Part 2 Assignment · PDF, 25 pages · Summer 2026

    The second observation, after the instruction. Questions 4 to 12 cover the rest of the course, set in the Part 1 context, and the two parts together were built to work as a final exam study guide.

    Teaches: One-Shot GenAI Resistance, iterative perturbation process

    From Perturbed Assignments and Subgoal-Labeled AI Literacy Instruction in Undergraduate Discrete Mathematics

Using these

The three procedures these materials teach each have a page of their own, with the work they came from and the work they build on: the hallucination autopsy, the four parts of a strong prompt, and collaborative prompting. The steps in all three are subgoal labels, which is why the same words appear in the reference box, the worked example and the student's own task.
