Concept

iterative perturbation process

The iterative perturbation process is an eight-step, instructor-led loop that rewrites an assignment question until a target chatbot fails it three times running.

Also written: Adversarial Iterative Perturbation · Adversarial Assignment Perturbation · iterative perturbation method

Defined in
Adversarial Assignment Perturbation: Effects on Help-Seeking Behaviors in Student Generative AI Chatbot Use, Table 1
DOI
https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29770-9_48
Tool
Perturbation Workbench

The eight steps run in a loop. Write a rubric for the unperturbed question, pick the model that matches what a student can actually reach, and test the original question against it. If the chatbot scores at or above 60%, apply one of the three perturbation strategies and try again against a fresh instance, so nothing carries over from the last exchange. Each pass also updates the rubric, so the hallucinations the model produced become things the grading scheme explicitly penalises.

A person has to drive this, and the paper says why: choosing which perturbation to apply relies on domain expertise to identify and target a specific failure point. That judgement is the part a script cannot supply.

When a question gets perturbed, the attempts recorded against its earlier form stay with that form, because they are evidence about that exact wording.
