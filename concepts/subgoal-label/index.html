Concept

Subgoal Label

A Subgoal Label names the function a step serves in any problem of its type, so a learner can separate a solution's structure from one example's surface details.

A construct from the computing education literature, where it was studied for procedures a learner performs. The three procedures on this site apply it to appraising a chatbot's solution instead, which is a different target.

Also written: subgoal labelling · subgoal-labeled instruction

A worked example shows a student one solved problem. The trouble is that a student looking at it cannot always tell which parts are the method and which parts are this particular question, so what they take away is the question.

A subgoal label names what a step is for. Not “substitute k+1”, but “test a small case” — a description that still makes sense on a problem with different numbers in it. The labels are repeated in three places on purpose: a box the student can refer to, a worked example where they can see the step being done, and the task where they do it themselves. Same label, same words, all three times.

The computing education work on this studied procedures a learner performs, and the procedures on this site point them somewhere else: at judging what a chatbot has written. That is a different target, and whether labels transfer to it is a question rather than a finding.

Nearby terms in the literature: worked example, cognitive load, schema acquisition. None of these is a synonym for Subgoal Label; they name related work this paper did not measure against.

Related terms

  • Hallucination Autopsy Procedure

    The Hallucination Autopsy Procedure is a seven-step routine for finding where a chatbot's proof leaves the claim it was given, and naming why it left.

  • Axiom Reversion

    Axiom Reversion is a failure mode in which a chatbot swaps in the textbook theorem that the problem was perturbed away from.

  • Invalid Syntactic Shortcut

    An Invalid Syntactic Shortcut is a failure mode in which a chatbot manipulates a form as though a rule applied, producing a step that is well-formed and false.

  • Spatial or Counting Miscount

    A Spatial or Counting Miscount is a failure mode in which a chatbot counts placements that break the constraint, or counts the same one twice.

  • Strong Prompt Structure

    Strong Prompt Structure is a four-part shape for a prompt: establish context, require justification at every step, build in self-questioning, and plan the correction.

  • Collaborative Prompting

    Collaborative Prompting is a six-step routine in which a student forbids the final answer, takes one step at a time, and checks each against cases computed by hand.
