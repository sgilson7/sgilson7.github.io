Three-Attempt Consistency Protocol

Definition

The Three-Attempt Consistency Protocol repeats the same question in three separate chatbot attempts before applying the study’s resistance criterion.

Explanation

One failed response may be an isolated outcome. The procedure therefore repeats the exact question and grades each response against the stated rubric. In the AIED work, all three scores had to be below 60% for the question to meet the criterion.

Use a fresh conversation for each attempt and record the model, access route, date, prompt version, and rubric. A prompt edit creates a new question version; its results should not be combined with the earlier wording as though they tested the same input.

The resulting record supports a claim about those three attempts. More trials and a broader set of conditions would be needed to estimate reliability beyond them.

Scope and evidence

This is a procedural consistency check, not a statistical guarantee or an independent validation of student learning.

Sources

Gilson, Tabarsi, and Barnes (2027; first online 2026). Adversarial Assignment Perturbation: Effects on Help-Seeking Behaviors in Student Generative AI Chatbot Use.
