Strong prompt structure

Definition

Strong prompt structure is a four-part teaching routine for stating context, requesting justification, examining assumptions, and planning corrections.

Explanation

A prompt can make the kind of help you want more explicit. For a proof, that may mean asking for one justified step, rather than a finished answer. The response still needs checking.

LabelPurposeAction
B1Establish contextState the subject, problem, allowed methods, and what kind of help you want.
B2Require step-by-step justificationAsk each step to identify the rule or earlier result it uses.
B3Build in self-questioningAsk the chatbot to state its assumptions and check them against the problem. Treat its self-check as another claim to examine.
B4Plan the corrective dialogueDecide how you will identify an error and ask for a revision. Quote the disputed step and explain what it conflicts with.

These parts give the student something concrete to ask and something concrete to inspect. They are an instructional routine, not a guarantee that the resulting answer will be correct.

Scope and evidence

Procedure B was taught in the ongoing Summer 2026 study and presented in the guest lecture. Its effect on learning or error detection has not been established. It should not be equated with a validated prompting method merely because it requests reasoning.
