Concept

Structured Unevenness

Structured Unevenness is the pattern where a model's accuracy varies by topic in the same way across vendors, rather than varying at random.

Measured on five models over all 12,500 MATH benchmark problems. MATH has been public since 2021, so the accuracies are read as upper bounds rather than as underlying ability.

Also written: subject-level variance · cross-vendor weakness profile

Introduced in
Sam Gilson. Free-tier Large Language Models on the MATH Benchmark: Implications for AI Literacy Instruction. AERA 2027 annual meeting.
Measured with
Dan Hendrycks, Collin Burns, Saurav Kadavath. 2021. Measuring Mathematical Problem Solving with the MATH Dataset. Proceedings of the NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks Track.The 12,500-problem MATH dataset, seven subjects by five difficulty levels.
Described in
Gilson, 2026 · Free-tier Large Language Models on the MATH Benchmark: Implications for AI Literacy Instruction, Results and Substantiated Conclusions

Five models, three companies, 12,500 problems each. The thing worth reporting is not that they get most of it right, though they do. It is that they get the same things wrong.

Within each model, accuracy moved by 9.3 to 14.0 percentage points across subjects. Intermediate algebra, precalculus and geometry occupied the bottom three positions for every one of the five, which is not what you would expect from three vendors training separately. The spread does not follow price or overall accuracy either: the widest-spread model was the second most accurate, not the cheapest.

Read as error rates it is sharper. Each model’s error rate on its weakest subject is at least double its rate on its strongest.

MATH has been public since 2021, so every model has likely seen it. These numbers are an upper bound on performance against genuinely new problems.

Nearby terms in the literature: benchmark contamination, capability profile. None of these is a synonym for Structured Unevenness; they name related work this paper did not measure against.

Related terms

  • Free-Tier Accessible Model

    A Free-Tier Accessible Model is one a student can reach through a consumer chatbot interface without a paid subscription.

  • Calibrated Trust

    Calibrated Trust is trust in a tool that matches the tool's actual reliability, rather than being uniform across everything the tool does.
