Concept

AI Literacy

AI Literacy is the set of competencies that let a person critically evaluate AI technologies, and communicate and collaborate effectively with AI.

A definition from the AI literacy literature. A review of 124 AI literacy studies found only two critical-literacy interventions for AI tools, neither deployed in an educational setting.

Students arrive already using chatbots. That is the starting condition, not a thing to be decided, so the question is what they can do with one.

The definition that has stuck covers two halves: being able to judge what the system produces, and being able to work with it. The first half is the one coursework tends to skip, because it is easier to teach someone to operate a tool than to disbelieve it.

There is a gap worth knowing about. A review of 124 AI literacy studies found two that taught critical evaluation of an AI tool, and neither of those ran in a classroom. So the literature is large and the part of it that has been put in front of students is small.

Nearby terms in the literature: digital literacy, critical evaluation of AI output, appropriate reliance. None of these is a synonym for AI Literacy; they name related work this paper did not measure against.

Related terms

  • Hallucination Autopsy Procedure

    The Hallucination Autopsy Procedure is a seven-step routine for finding where a chatbot's proof leaves the claim it was given, and naming why it left.

  • Axiom Reversion

    Axiom Reversion is a failure mode in which a chatbot swaps in the textbook theorem that the problem was perturbed away from.

  • Invalid Syntactic Shortcut

    An Invalid Syntactic Shortcut is a failure mode in which a chatbot manipulates a form as though a rule applied, producing a step that is well-formed and false.

  • Spatial or Counting Miscount

    A Spatial or Counting Miscount is a failure mode in which a chatbot counts placements that break the constraint, or counts the same one twice.

  • Strong Prompt Structure

    Strong Prompt Structure is a four-part shape for a prompt: establish context, require justification at every step, build in self-questioning, and plan the correction.

  • Collaborative Prompting

    Collaborative Prompting is a six-step routine in which a student forbids the final answer, takes one step at a time, and checks each against cases computed by hand.

  • GenAI-Assisted Prototyping Procedure

    The GenAI-Assisted Prototyping Procedure is a nine-stage pipeline, brief to handoff, in which a person reads the plan, plays the build, and deploys it.

  • Subgoal Label

    A Subgoal Label names the function a step serves in any problem of its type, so a learner can separate a solution's structure from one example's surface details.
