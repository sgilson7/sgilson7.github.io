Concept

GenAI dependence

GenAI dependence is an engagement pattern in which a student accepts a chatbot's hallucinated answer without critically evaluating it.

A component label from one exploratory analysis of 8 students' chat logs in a discrete mathematics course. It is one of a contrastive pair with Cognitive Scaffolding, and the two came out of the same analysis.

GenAI dependence describes what a student does when they accept the chatbot’s output without testing it.

Dependence is the other component the Independent Component Analysis separated out of the same 8 students’ discrete mathematics transcripts, and it characterises the style of information seeking. Its negative pole loads on Content_Elaboration, open-ended requests for assistance; its positive pole on Writing_Format, Writing_Instructions and Content_Example, which are rigid and highly specific requests for structure or answers.

Among the same 8 students, the lower-scoring ones — group mean perturbed assignment grade 81.3 — did not form one uniform cluster. The paper describes two distinct failing behaviours: a student who demanded formats and examples, constraining the output format while leaving the logical content alone; and a student who asked for generic advice without supplying context, and accepted advice that missed the perturbations entirely.

Two of the 8 sit outside the pattern, and the paper keeps them there. One scored highly while clustering near the low performers, because they had already solved the problem and were using the chatbot to check formatting — their low engagement was efficiency. Another scored positively on scaffolding but lacked the prior knowledge to evaluate the justification, and accepted hallucinated axioms as truth.

This is a case study of eight students in one class over one semester, and two of those eight do not fit the pattern.

Nearby terms in the literature: cognitive offloading, dependent offloading, executive help-seeking, over-reliance on AI. None of these is a synonym for GenAI dependence; they name related work this paper did not measure against.

Related terms

  • One-Shot GenAI Resistance

    One-Shot GenAI Resistance is a property of an assignment question: a target chatbot scores below 60% on it across three separate attempts with the same prompt.

  • iterative perturbation process

    The iterative perturbation process is an eight-step, instructor-led loop that rewrites an assignment question until a target chatbot fails it three times running.

  • Spatial Injection

    Spatial Injection is a perturbation strategy that adds reasoning requiring visual or geometric interpretation to a question, so a model must track layout as well as arithmetic.

  • Axiomatic Replacement

    Axiomatic Replacement is a perturbation strategy that alters a question's fundamental logical premises or axioms to prevent a model retrieving a memorised proof.

  • Contextual Embedding

    Contextual Embedding is a perturbation strategy that places a question inside unfamiliar, class-specific context which the grading rubric then requires the answer to use.

  • Three-Attempt Consistency Protocol

    The Three-Attempt Consistency Protocol re-prompts a model with the exact query two further times, and classifies a question resistant only if all three attempts fail.

  • Cognitive Scaffolding

    Cognitive Scaffolding is an engagement pattern in which a student recognises that a chatbot has hallucinated and supplies context to steer it toward a correct answer.

  • DRIVE framework

    The DRIVE framework is a taxonomy for generative AI interactions that separates how a student steers an exchange from how they articulate what they know.
