Concept

DRIVE framework

The DRIVE framework is a taxonomy for generative AI interactions that separates how a student steers an exchange from how they articulate what they know.

Also written: Deep Reasoning in Virtual Education · DRIVE taxonomy

Defined in
Adversarial Assignment Perturbation: Effects on Help-Seeking Behaviors in Student Generative AI Chatbot Use, Section 2, applying Oliveira et al. (2025)
DOI
https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29770-9_48

DRIVE is the work of Oliveira, Zednik, Bombaerts, Sadowski and Conijn, "Assessing students' DRIVE: a framework to evaluate learning through interactions with generative AI", Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence 9, 100497 (2025), doi:10.1016/j.caeai.2025.100497.

DRIVE distinguishes Directive Reasoning, how a student steers an interaction, from Visible Expertise, how they articulate what they know. That split lets engagement be separated into functional dimensions — content-building, such as asking for a conceptual definition; argumentation, such as refining a proof; and writing processes, such as asking for syntactic formatting.

The AIED 2026 study adopts DRIVE to code student queries during a One-Shot GenAI-Resistant assignment, segmenting chat logs into interaction turns and coding each query-response pair by hand. Because the taxonomy has 36 possible tags, the study applied an Independent Component Analysis over them to find latent structure, which is where Cognitive Scaffolding and GenAI dependence came from.
