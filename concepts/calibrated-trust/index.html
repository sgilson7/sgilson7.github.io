Concept

Calibrated Trust

Calibrated Trust is trust in a tool that matches the tool's actual reliability, rather than being uniform across everything the tool does.

A construct from the human-AI interaction literature. Here it is the instructional target a subject-level reliability profile is meant to serve, which is a design argument rather than a measured effect.

A student gets a wrong answer from a chatbot on a geometry problem. What they conclude from that is the whole question.

If they decide they are bad at geometry, nothing about how they use the tool changes. If they decide the tool is weaker at geometry, their checking changes shape: more verification there, less elsewhere. Calibrated trust is the second reading, and it needs something to calibrate against — which is an argument for publishing what the tools are measurably weak at, rather than telling students to be sceptical in general.

Confidence of tone is not evidence. A chatbot sounds the same on the subject it is worst at.

Nearby terms in the literature: automation bias, appropriate reliance, overreliance. None of these is a synonym for Calibrated Trust; they name related work this paper did not measure against.

Related terms

  • Free-Tier Accessible Model

    A Free-Tier Accessible Model is one a student can reach through a consumer chatbot interface without a paid subscription.

  • Structured Unevenness

    Structured Unevenness is the pattern where a model's accuracy varies by topic in the same way across vendors, rather than varying at random.
