Concept

Cognitive Scaffolding

Cognitive Scaffolding is an engagement pattern in which a student recognises that a chatbot has hallucinated and supplies context to steer it toward a correct answer.

Also written: cognitive scaffolding strategy · collaborative repair

Defined in
Adversarial Assignment Perturbation: Effects on Help-Seeking Behaviors in Student Generative AI Chatbot Use, Sections 3.2 and 3.3
DOI
https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29770-9_48

These are the students who catch the machine being wrong and do something about it.

In the AIED 2026 case study of 8 students who used the chatbot, Cognitive Scaffolding emerged as one of two statistically independent components in an Independent Component Analysis of the coded chat logs. It loads on Argument_Justify (asking the machine for reasons for a claim), Argument_Structure (asking it to impose a logical structure), and Content_Critical (engaging critically with what it produced, asking for clarification or correction).

Among those 8 students, the higher-scoring ones clustered on this component, with a group mean perturbed assignment grade of 94.6. The paper describes the pattern as collaborative repair: treating the model’s limitations as constraints to be managed through corrective dialogue. Within that sample of 8, the paper reports a significant correlation between the two engagement patterns and the perturbed assignment grades.

Eight students, one class, one semester. That is the size of the claim. The grouping came from an exploratory component analysis on those eight, and the paper says plainly that future work must scale the protocol to larger populations before the stability of these patterns can be treated as settled.
