Cognitive scaffolding

Definition

In the AIED case study, cognitive scaffolding names an interaction pattern in which a student recognized an incorrect chatbot response and supplied context or criticism to improve it.

Explanation

A useful question when reading a chat log is what the student does after the chatbot makes an error. The student may identify a missing assumption, ask for justification, or explain why a step does not follow. Those actions give us more evidence about the interaction than the final answer alone.

The AIED study examined eight students who used an optional chatbot. It identified cognitive scaffolding and GenAI dependence as engagement patterns and reported an association with assignment grades within that sample.

The term also has a broader history in education. Here it identifies the paper’s use of the label; it should not be read as a claim that every scaffolded interaction has been classified or that repairing an answer necessarily produces learning.

Scope and evidence

This is an exploratory finding from one course. A transcript records expressed reasoning, not every part of a student’s understanding. The association with grades is not a causal effect.
