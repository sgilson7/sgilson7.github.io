Concepts

This guide explains the concepts used in my research and teaching. Some are established ideas from the literature; others are labels used in a particular study or teaching routine. Each page identifies that scope and links to its sources.

Browse the concepts

Designing and testing assignments

Iterative perturbation process — Iterative assignment perturbation is an instructor-led process of revising a question and testing a target chatbot’s responses against a rubric.

One-Shot GenAI Resistance — In this research, One-Shot GenAI Resistance means that a specified chatbot scores below the study’s rubric threshold on three attempts with the same question.

Three-Attempt Consistency Protocol — The Three-Attempt Consistency Protocol repeats the same question in three separate chatbot attempts before applying the study’s resistance criterion.

Spatial injection — Spatial injection adds a visual or geometric constraint to a question as part of assignment perturbation.

Axiomatic replacement — Axiomatic replacement changes or explicitly restates the premises and rules a solution is allowed to use.

Contextual embedding — Contextual embedding places a question in a specified setting and requires the solution to use that setting’s definitions or constraints.

Examining student interactions

DRIVE framework — DRIVE is a framework and taxonomy for examining how students direct a generative AI interaction and express their expertise within it.

Cognitive scaffolding — In the AIED case study, cognitive scaffolding names an interaction pattern in which a student recognized an incorrect chatbot response and supplied context or criticism to improve it.

GenAI dependence — In the AIED case study, GenAI dependence names an engagement pattern involving acceptance of incorrect chatbot output without critical evaluation visible in the interaction.

Online, on-demand CS coursework — Online, on-demand CS coursework is computing instruction learners access online and work through on their own schedule.

Teaching and checking reasoning

AI literacy — AI literacy concerns the competencies people use to evaluate AI, work with it, and use it in their lives.

Hallucination autopsy procedure — The hallucination autopsy procedure is a teaching routine for checking a chatbot’s proof, locating an error, and examining a possible explanation for it.

Axiom reversion — Axiom reversion is the teaching label used here when a response substitutes a familiar theorem or formula for the one specified in the problem.

Invalid syntactic shortcut — An invalid syntactic shortcut is a proof step that looks like a familiar manipulation but is not licensed by the relevant rule or assumptions.

Spatial or counting miscount — A spatial or counting miscount occurs when a solution includes forbidden configurations, misses valid ones, or counts a configuration more than once.

Strong prompt structure — Strong prompt structure is a four-part teaching routine for stating context, requesting justification, examining assumptions, and planning corrections.

Collaborative prompting — Collaborative prompting is a teaching routine for working through a problem with a chatbot while keeping responsibility for checking and explaining the reasoning.

Subgoal label — A subgoal label names the purpose of a group of solution steps so a learner can recognize that purpose in another problem.

Capabilities and development practice

Free-tier accessible model — A free-tier accessible model is a model available through an access route that does not require a paid subscription at the time of use.

Structured unevenness — Structured unevenness is the descriptive label used here for a pattern of model performance that varies systematically across subject areas.

Calibrated trust — Calibrated trust is trust that is appropriately related to evidence about a system’s capabilities and limitations in a particular task.

GenAI-assisted prototyping procedure — The GenAI-assisted prototyping procedure is a development workflow that combines a coding agent’s output with human review, testing, play, and handoff.
