Concept

Hallucination Autopsy Procedure

The Hallucination Autopsy Procedure is a seven-step routine for finding where a chatbot's proof leaves the claim it was given, and naming why it left.

A teaching routine from one guest lecture, written to be done by hand on one proof at a time. It has not been evaluated as an intervention.

Also written: hallucination autopsy · Procedure A

Defined in
Gilson, 2026 · Applications of AI Literacy to GenAI-Assisted Game Development, Procedure A, slide 17

A student reads a proof a chatbot has written, decides it looks fine, and moves on. It usually does look fine. The sentences are the right shape and the notation is the right notation, and the error is one line in the middle.

The procedure is seven steps done by hand on one proof. Before reading, write down how much you expect to trust the answer for this kind of problem. Then restate the claim exactly as given. Find the rule the chatbot actually substituted, which is rarely the one it says it used. Mark the line where that rule and the stated hypothesis part company. Evaluate both sides at a small value of n, and if the numbers happen to agree, check a derivation step anyway. Name the failure. Then say why that failure was likely, which means connecting it to how the text was generated rather than to carelessness.

The first and last steps are the pair that matter. You guess how much to trust it, and afterwards you find out whether you were right.

Nearby terms in the literature: error analysis, self-explanation, critical evaluation of AI output. None of these is a synonym for Hallucination Autopsy Procedure; they name related work this paper did not measure against.

Related terms

  • Axiom Reversion

    Axiom Reversion is a failure mode in which a chatbot swaps in the textbook theorem that the problem was perturbed away from.

  • Invalid Syntactic Shortcut

    An Invalid Syntactic Shortcut is a failure mode in which a chatbot manipulates a form as though a rule applied, producing a step that is well-formed and false.

  • Spatial or Counting Miscount

    A Spatial or Counting Miscount is a failure mode in which a chatbot counts placements that break the constraint, or counts the same one twice.

  • Strong Prompt Structure

    Strong Prompt Structure is a four-part shape for a prompt: establish context, require justification at every step, build in self-questioning, and plan the correction.

  • Collaborative Prompting

    Collaborative Prompting is a six-step routine in which a student forbids the final answer, takes one step at a time, and checks each against cases computed by hand.

  • GenAI-Assisted Prototyping Procedure

    The GenAI-Assisted Prototyping Procedure is a nine-stage pipeline, brief to handoff, in which a person reads the plan, plays the build, and deploys it.
