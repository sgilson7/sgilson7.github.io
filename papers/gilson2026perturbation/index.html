Conference paper

Adversarial Assignment Perturbation: Effects on Help-Seeking Behaviors in Student Generative AI Chatbot Use

Sam Gilson, Benyamin Tabarsi and Tiffany Barnes

Artificial Intelligence in Education (AIED 2026), LNAI 16585, pp. 440-449

Published
27 June 2026, in a 2027 volume
DOI
10.1007/978-3-032-29770-9_48

Abstract

The widespread adoption of generative AI (GenAI) tools in educational settings necessitates methods to differentiate student-AI interactions indicative of productive help-seeking from shallow cognitive outsourcing. This work conducts a mixed-methods analysis of GenAI queries and learning in an undergraduate discrete mathematics course. Participants were provided with an optional, GenAI-powered chatbot to aid them while completing a One-Shot GenAI-Resistant assignment, generated via an iterative perturbation process. We conducted a case study of 8 students who used the chatbot, and found two disparate engagement patterns: cognitive scaffolding, recognizing GenAI chatbot had hallucinated and provided additional context to leverage the chatbot to discover the correct answer, and GenAI dependence behaviors, which failed to critically evaluate hallucinated GenAI chatbot answers. Within this small sample, we found a significant correlation between cognitive scaffolding and GenAI dependent engagement patterns and the perturbed assignment grades. These findings demonstrate that assignment perturbation, combined with chat log analysis, can be used to differentiate deep and shallow GenAI engagement strategies.
