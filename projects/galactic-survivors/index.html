Galactic Survivors

Game

Galactic Survivors is a survivors-like that runs in a browser: you land on an alien planet and survive twelve minutes of waves from its native creatures, then fight the hive's Matriarch. It started from a recorded conversation between two players of Vampire Survivors and Halls of Torment, who talked through what makes these games work while they played. I read the transcript for those points and turned each one into a rule the game enforces and a test checks.

The main one is that, moment to moment, the whole job is moving. Your abilities aim and fire on their own, nothing needs reloading, and the camera never turns or shakes. The screen is kept calm on purpose: a dark ground, bright round characters, and one shape for every enemy shot, drawn on top of everything else. An enemy can hurt you at most once in a short window, so a crowd pressed against you lands one hit, not ten, and close-range fighting stays worth doing. No enemy is immune to anything, so no character is stuck against a boss. When an ability can evolve, the next level-up offers the evolution as a card.

There are three characters: Pathfinder, whose shoulder drone aims for you; Breaker, who wades into the crowd and takes half damage from contact; and Vector, who is fast and fragile, aims with the pointer, and can dash. Rifts open during a run and take you into a small arena against one elite. Partway through, a corporate raid may land drones, troopers and a walking mech. Between runs you keep your gold, buy permanent upgrades, and spend tickets in a salvage cache for modules, with the odds on the screen and a rare certain within ten pulls. All twenty achievements are listed from the start with your progress, and each adds a little experience to every later run.

Two players can play the same run, each in their own browser, joined by a room code or by a pasted invitation. Both browsers run the whole run from the same seed and inputs, so neither waits on a server. Every rule runs in a Rust core in integer maths, compiled to WebAssembly, and the art is drawn in TikZ.

Playing it

WASD or the arrow keys move, and the mouse aims the few abilities that use it. Press 1, 2 or 3 to choose a level-up card, R for a new hand, Space to dash as Vector, and Esc to pause. Your progress is a file: after a run, download your save, and load it next time. The page keeps nothing in your browser.

Checked in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit.
