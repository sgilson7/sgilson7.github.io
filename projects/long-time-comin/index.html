Long Time Comin'

Game

Long Time Comin' is a third-person shooter that runs in a browser, in the bullet-hell style of Returnal and Saros: enemies fire large orbs in rings, spirals and flowers, and you dodge through them while shooting back. In the story you committed a crime in 2081 that the court calls the Stilling, and were sentenced to go back and stop yourself. The court's Bailiff sent you fifty years too early, so you land in 2031 and have to cross the half century on foot.

Each run crosses the first zone, the Waiting City, as a graph of arenas. One main path runs from the crater where you land to the Court of Hours and its boss, the Bailiff of Hours. Side paths of one or two harder fights leave the main path and rejoin it, and each ends in a reward shown on the map before you choose: a higher level of one of the three guns, more health, more speed, another jump (up to four), or one of three that last the run: shots that pierce, a dash that clears the orbs round you, or a parry that heals. Before the boss, the Antechamber offers half your health back or one more reward, and you take one. Every run rolls its own graph. When you die, the court reads the next of the ten counts of your indictment and adds years to your sentence, and you return to the Holding, where the Clemency the run earned buys upgrades. Two of them add mechanics: a grappling hook that hauls you to what it hits and keeps your speed when you let go, and a parry that turns gold orbs back at whoever fired them. The route has three bosses and a final one; zones two to four are sealed in this version.

The arenas have height: bridges and walkways to climb or run under, towers, and broken walls to hide behind, and in the boss's court, stands that its ground waves cannot reach. An in-game Codex lists every control, every level of every gun, every enemy and the orbs each attack fires, every upgrade and the rules, all written by the core from the numbers the game runs on.

Every rule runs in a Rust core in fixed-point integer maths, compiled to WebAssembly, and the page draws what the core returns. Each enemy pattern is data: how many orbs a volley holds, how far each repeat turns, and how fast each orb curls or changes speed. The arenas are built from a seed, with lit windows, rooftop clutter and a clock tower stopped at 21:14,, and every shape is outlined in one plum ink to match the menu art, which is drawn in TikZ.

Playing it

W, A, S and D move and the mouse aims. Hold the left button to fire. Space jumps, and again in the air if you have the jumps; Shift dashes, and orbs pass through you while you dash. E takes a gun from the floor, Tab opens the map and Escape pauses. Your record is kept in the browser you played in.
