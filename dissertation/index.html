Dissertation

Students can hand almost any homework question to a chatbot now and get back something that looks like an answer. That leaves an instructor with a problem they cannot see. A submitted assignment looks the same whether a student reasoned their way to it or copied it down.

The fix has to happen in the assignment. Change the question until the chatbot’s answer is wrong in a way a student can detect, hand it to the class, and record what they do with the wrong answer.

Some students catch it and some do not. Checking an AI’s reasoning is a skill like any other in the course, which means it can be taught directly instead of being assumed.

So the work runs in that order: make the behaviour visible, then teach it.

Where it started

Before any of this I wanted to know why students were studying outside their degree programmes at all, and why they stopped. That survey of 51 undergraduates, four of whom I interviewed, found that a common reason for giving up on online, on-demand coursework was that a large language model had replaced it. That is the behaviour the rest of the work goes looking for, in a setting where the transcript is available to read.

What is published

Making reliance observable. The first study built a method for rewriting a discrete mathematics question until a chatbot fails it, then ran the resulting questions as a two-week lab in a Summer 2025 discrete mathematics course of 18 students, 8 of whom used the chatbot they were offered. Two engagement patterns separated out of those 8 transcripts. It is published at AIED 2026, and the terms it introduced each have a page here.

What is in progress

Three further phases are under way. Two are with reviewers or in analysis and one is a design, so there is nothing to show yet and no findings to report. What I can say about their shape:

  • A survey of what the free chatbots students actually reach can and cannot do on school and early university mathematics.
  • A classroom study of teaching students to check generative AI output on unfamiliar problems.
  • A comparison design testing whether the instruction transfers.

Each will get a page here when it is accepted, and not before.
